\vspace*{2in}
\noindent\textbf{About this book.} This is a metaphysics-only work. Its
subject is the relation between the fundamental nature of reality, as
articulated by the Islamic metaphysical tradition (most centrally Ibn
\arb{Arabi}, Mulla \arb{Sadra}, and Shah \arb{Wali Allah}), and the
economic phenomena we observe. It treats classical Islamic economic
concepts --- \arb{niyyah}, \arb{qadar}, \arb{rizq}, \arb{barakah},
\arb{halal}, \arb{haram}, \arb{riba} --- not as objects of mathematical
modeling but as objects of metaphysical inquiry. The question is not
\textit{how to model these phenomena} but \textit{what these phenomena
are at the level of being}.

\vspace{0.3in}
\noindent\textbf{What this book is not.} It is not a textbook of Islamic
economics. It does not present formal microeconomic theory; that is the
work of a subsequent volume. It does not present ergodic, network-theoretic,
or behavioral treatments of Islamic economic concepts; those treatments,
which use modern applied apparatus to model the dynamics of \arb{riba},
\arb{rizq} networks, and gratitude effects, belong to a separate companion
volume in heterodox economics. They are valuable but they are not
metaphysics.

\vspace{0.3in}
\noindent\textbf{The conceptual apparatus} is restricted to frameworks
with genuine scientific authority. Two are load-bearing: quantum
foundations (the measurement problem, the live interpretations,
non-locality as established by the Bell experiments) and mathematical
logic via the Penrose-G\"odel argument (the non-algorithmic character of
mathematical understanding established from the incompleteness theorems
and the halting problem). The Akbarian metaphysical tradition is then
articulated in its own terms within the categorial space these two
frameworks open. Broader philosophical traditions (Whitehead's process
philosophy, Heideggerian phenomenology, contemporary analytical idealism)
are mentioned in passing where they illuminate but are not treated as
load-bearing apparatus.

\vspace{0.3in}
\noindent\textit{Comments, criticisms, and corrections are sought. The
work is intended as a contribution to a project that has barely begun;
its development requires the engagement of others.}
