## LinkedIn

The Solow growth model is economics' workhorse for understanding national growth. Its core insight: capital accumulation alone can't sustain long-term growth due to diminishing returns. Only technological progress drives lasting prosperity. The model makes a bold prediction: poor countries should grow faster than rich ones and eventually converge. Reality tells a different story. Pakistan's per-capita growth has been anemic for decades. Malaysia succeeded. Indonesia performed well. The pattern isn't about Islam or culture—it's about institutions, governance, and critically, human capital. The Islamic intellectual tradition has always emphasized 'ilm' (knowledge) as fundamental. This should be a natural advantage, yet many Muslim-majority countries haven't fully leveraged this in their modern development strategies. When we extend the Solow model to include human capital, the convergence story changes dramatically. Knowledge compounds without diminishing returns.
