Why does a worker in Germany produce ten times more per hour than a worker in Bangladesh? It's not effort—it's capital, technology, and institutions. For five centuries, the Islamic world was the global productivity leader. Baghdad, Cordoba, and Cairo were centers of innovation in agriculture, manufacturing, and finance. The production function was working. The decline wasn't just colonialism. Internal institutional failures, loss of intellectual dynamism, and resistance to new technologies all played a role. Today, Muslim-majority countries are disproportionately in the lower-middle and low-income categories. The honest diagnosis: low total factor productivity, weak institutions, underinvestment.
