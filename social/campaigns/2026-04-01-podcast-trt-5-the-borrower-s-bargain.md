## LinkedIn

Government borrowing isn't really about accessing capital. It's about risk allocation. When governments issue conventional bonds, they're making a promise that future taxpayers will honor the debt regardless of project outcomes. The government gets immediate funding, but citizens inherit the obligation. Revenue-backed financing works differently. The government promises that specific revenue streams will service the debt. If the project fails to generate returns, the financing arrangement itself bears the loss. Islamic public finance prohibits interest-bearing debt entirely. This constraint initially seems limiting, but it creates genuine fiscal discipline. Governments can't simply promise to tax their way out of problems—they must structure financing around projects that generate measurable value. The substance versus form debate in sukuk markets reflects this tension between genuine risk-sharing and conventional debt dressed in Islamic clothing.
