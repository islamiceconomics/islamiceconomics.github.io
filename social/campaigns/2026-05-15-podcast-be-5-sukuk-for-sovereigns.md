## LinkedIn

In 2014, the United Kingdom quietly pioneered something remarkable: the first Western sovereign sukuk, raising £200 million backed by government property rather than debt. This wasn't just financial innovation—it was a fundamental shift in how governments can raise capital. Instead of borrowing money at interest, the UK sold investors shares in real government assets. Malaysia has since built the world's largest sukuk market with over $200 billion outstanding. Hong Kong, Luxembourg, and South Africa followed suit. The difference matters: conventional bonds make taxpayers debtors to bondholders. Sukuk makes them asset co-owners, earning returns from the revenue those assets generate.
