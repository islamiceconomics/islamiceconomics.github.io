## LinkedIn

For centuries, the Ottoman Empire operated as the world's largest economy without sovereign debt. Their financial system relied on cash waqfs that provided regulated lending to merchants, while wars were funded through taxation and conquest rather than borrowing. Then came 1854 and the Crimean War. The Ottomans issued their first foreign loan from European banks. This single decision triggered a financial cascade that would destroy the empire from within. Within two decades, debt payments consumed over half the state's revenue. By 1881, European creditors had established the Ottoman Public Debt Administration, effectively seizing control of the empire's entire financial system. This episode traces how the transition from Islamic financial principles to European-style sovereign debt contributed to the collapse of the last great Islamic empire.
