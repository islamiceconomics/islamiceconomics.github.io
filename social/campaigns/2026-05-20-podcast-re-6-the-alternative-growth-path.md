## LinkedIn

What happens when you change the fundamental assumptions about human behavior in economic models? This fascinating exploration examines how different preference axioms could create entirely different growth paths. The theoretical framework suggests: • Different savings behavior patterns • Equity-based rather than debt-based investment allocation • Intrinsically motivated rather than purely incentive-driven labor • Knowledge-driven institutional structures • More accountable governance systems The predicted outcome? Potentially slower but more stable growth, more equitably distributed, with fewer boom-bust cycles and reduced financialization. But here's the crucial caveat: this remains largely theoretical. No country has implemented a comprehensive alternative economic system based on these principles. We're still at the "interesting hypothesis" stage rather than the "proven theory" stage. The research agenda ahead involves formalizing these preference axioms, finding natural experiments, and building empirical evidence for what sounds theoretically elegant but remains empirically untested.
