In 696 CE, Caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan made a decision that would reshape global commerce: he minted the first purely Islamic gold dinar, replacing Byzantine and Sassanid coins across the caliphate. This wasn't just currency reform. It was a declaration of monetary sovereignty. The Islamic bimetallic system that emerged was remarkably sophisticated: • Standardized weights (the mithqal) • Quality control through the muhtasib • Proto-forex markets run by sarrafs • Mint networks spanning three continents Islamic civilization didn't just have money. It had a monetary system with rules, institutions, and global reach. The system faced real challenges—Mamluk debasement, silver-gold ratio instability—but it functioned for centuries across vast distances.
