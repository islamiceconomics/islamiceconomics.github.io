## LinkedIn

The Quran bans riba, but defining its exact boundaries sparked centuries of scholarly debate among Islamic jurists. The four major schools of Islamic law each drew different lines: • The Hanafis permitted bay' bi-thaman ajil — selling goods on credit at higher prices — while strictly forbidding lending money for a return • The Hanbalis took a stricter stance, viewing any time-value premium with suspicion as potentially concealing riba • The Shafi'is focused their analysis on six specific commodities mentioned in prophetic traditions • The Malikis developed the concept of sadd al-dhara'i — blocking the means to forbidden ends These 8th-century debates weren't merely academic exercises. They established the theological and legal foundations that continue to shape Islamic banking products and contracts today. The question remains as relevant now as it was then: where exactly does legitimate commerce end and prohibited interest begin?
