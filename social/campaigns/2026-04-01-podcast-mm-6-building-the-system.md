## LinkedIn

The conversation around Islamic monetary systems often focuses on technical details—gold standards, currency baskets, or Shariah compliance frameworks. But the real challenge lies elsewhere entirely. Gold dinar revival proposals face a binding constraint that has nothing to do with monetary design. The principles exist. The capital exists. The technology exists. The need is demonstrably real across the Islamic world. What's missing is something much harder to engineer: trust between governments that have rarely cooperated at this institutional scale. Building an Islamic monetary system isn't fundamentally a monetary problem—it's a governance problem. Five clear design principles exist for an Islamic monetary authority. The question of OIC monetary cooperation has technical solutions. But creating the institutions that make sustained cooperation work between sovereign nations remains the ultimate challenge.
