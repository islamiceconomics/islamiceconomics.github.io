## LinkedIn

Most people think economics began with Adam Smith in 1776. It actually began with Ibn Khaldun describing supply and demand in 14th-century Tunis—four hundred years earlier. The Prophet Muhammad was a merchant before he was a prophet. Abu Yusuf wrote about market regulation. Al-Ghazali explained the division of labor. These weren't abstract theories developed in isolation. They were practical solutions for managing vast trade networks that connected Mecca to China. Islamic scholars were mapping economic principles while Europe was still emerging from the Dark Ages. They understood money, markets, and human behavior because they lived it daily in the bustling souks of Baghdad, Cairo, and Cordoba. We've forgotten an entire tradition of economic thought that preceded and influenced what we now call classical economics.
