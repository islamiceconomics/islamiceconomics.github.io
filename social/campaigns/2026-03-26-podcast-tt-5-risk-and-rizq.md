## LinkedIn

The word "hazard" comes from al-zahr, Arabic for dice — a reminder that Islamic civilization gave us not just goods and gold, but the very language of uncertainty. Medieval Muslim merchants faced a fundamental question: how do you build trust across vast distances when you're trading in things you cannot fully predict or control? Their answer shaped concepts we still use today: risk, hazard, and the ethics of uncertainty in contracts. The Islamic principle of gharar — excessive uncertainty that corrupts agreements — wasn't just legal theory. It was practical wisdom that helped merchants distinguish between acceptable business risk and speculative gambling. From mudaraba partnerships to modern takaful insurance (now a $50 billion industry), these frameworks continue to offer insights for contemporary finance.
