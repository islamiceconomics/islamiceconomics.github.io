## LinkedIn

In pre-Islamic Arabia, debt was more than a financial obligation — it was a path to slavery. A man who borrowed a camel and couldn't repay would see his debt double, then quadruple, then multiply again. The practice of riba al-jahiliyyah, literally "the usury of the Age of Ignorance," meant that missed payments could cost you your family's freedom. Children became collateral. Entire households were enslaved for debts that grew beyond any possibility of repayment. When the Quran was revealed, it didn't just prohibit interest — it dismantled a system of financial oppression that had trapped generations in cycles of bondage. This transformation of the creditor-debtor relationship remains one of Islam's most revolutionary economic contributions. Listen: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-wd-1
