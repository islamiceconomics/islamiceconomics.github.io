## LinkedIn

In 696 CE, Caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan made a revolutionary decision: he minted the first purely Islamic gold dinar, replacing Byzantine and Sassanid coins across the caliphate. This wasn't just currency reform—it was a declaration of monetary sovereignty. The Islamic bimetallic system that emerged was remarkably sophisticated. It featured standardized weights (the mithqal), quality control through the muhtasib, proto-forex markets run by sarrafs, and mint networks spanning three continents. The system had its struggles—Mamluk debasement, silver-gold ratio instability—but the key insight remains: Islamic civilization didn't just have money. It had a complete monetary system with rules, institutions, and oversight. Listen to the full analysis: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-mm-1
