## LinkedIn

Global debt has surpassed $300 trillion, yet alternative models exist that challenge our assumptions about lending and borrowing. Qard hasan, the "beautiful loan" in Islamic finance, expects no interest—only the return of principal. Sukuk instruments transform traditional debt into shared ownership of tangible assets. Diminishing musharakah allows homebuyers to gradually purchase their property from the bank as a partner rather than as a debtor. From microfinance experiments in Bangladesh to sovereign sukuk issued by the UK and Hong Kong, these principles are already being tested globally. The 7th-century insight that debt should serve people rather than enslave them offers a compelling framework for addressing our current debt crisis. As we grapple with unprecedented global debt levels, perhaps it's time to seriously consider financial systems designed around human flourishing rather than extraction.
