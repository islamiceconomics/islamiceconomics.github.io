## LinkedIn

The Solow growth model remains the workhorse of growth economics, built on a simple premise: economies produce output using capital and labor, with savings driving investment. But the model's most striking prediction—that poor countries should grow faster than rich ones due to diminishing returns—hasn't held for many Muslim-majority nations. Pakistan's per-capita growth has been anemic for decades. Indonesia performed better. Malaysia succeeded remarkably. The pattern isn't about Islamic principles but about institutions and governance. When we extend the model to include human capital, Islam's emphasis on ilm (knowledge) should theoretically provide an advantage. Yet convergence failures persist across many Muslim-majority countries, pointing to deeper structural challenges in how knowledge translates into economic growth.
