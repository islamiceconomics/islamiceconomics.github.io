## LinkedIn

In the 9th century, Baghdad's jahbadh bankers solved a problem that still challenges global finance today: how to move wealth across vast distances safely and efficiently. Their solution was the sakk, a bill of exchange that enabled merchants to trade from Spain to China without carrying gold coins. But the real innovation wasn't the paper—it was the hawala trust networks and mudaraba profit-sharing partnerships that made it work. A merchant in Damascus could write a sakk, and his partner in Delhi would honor it months later, sight unseen. This required something more valuable than gold: reputation that could travel 4,000 miles. This medieval Islamic financial system became the foundation for modern banking. The English word 'cheque' comes directly from sakk. Yet we've somehow forgotten the trust networks that made the whole system possible.
