## LinkedIn

One of the most counterintuitive findings in development economics is the resource curse: countries that discover oil or minerals often end up worse off, not better. The mechanism is well-documented—resource wealth weakens institutions, concentrates power, and crowds out productive sectors through Dutch disease. Only a few countries like Norway and Botswana have escaped this trap. What did they do differently? They built strong institutions before the resource boom hit. Islamic economics offers an interesting perspective through the fay' principle: communal resources belong to the community, not the state. The Gulf's sovereign wealth funds represent a partial modern application of this concept. With Pakistan sitting on significant critical minerals reserves, we have a real-time test case for whether these principles can translate into practice.
