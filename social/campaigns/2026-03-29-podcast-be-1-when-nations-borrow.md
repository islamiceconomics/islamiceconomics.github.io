## LinkedIn

Classical Islamic economists had clear views on government borrowing. Abu Yusuf argued that the state treasury (bayt al-mal) should be managed with the same principles that govern individual finances: no riba, no gharar, no burdening future generations with obligations they didn't consent to. Today, every nation on earth borrows money. The United States owes over $34 trillion. Japan's debt-to-GDP ratio exceeds 260%. This represents a fundamental departure from the economic vision of early Islamic jurists, who debated whether a caliph could even borrow on behalf of the ummah. The contrast between their principles and our reality raises profound questions about sovereignty, consent, and intergenerational justice in modern economics.
