The petrodollar paradox reveals something fascinating about modern Islamic finance. After 1973, Gulf states accumulated unprecedented oil wealth. Much flowed into US Treasury bonds and Western financial markets - interest-bearing instruments that conflict with Islamic prohibitions on riba. Meanwhile, non-oil Muslim-majority countries borrowed from those same Western markets at commercial rates. But the story isn't simple betrayal. The same Gulf states built massive development institutions: the Islamic Development Bank ($150+ billion in financing), the Kuwait Fund, and the Saudi Development Fund. They simultaneously integrated into the global financial system while funding infrastructure across the Muslim world.
