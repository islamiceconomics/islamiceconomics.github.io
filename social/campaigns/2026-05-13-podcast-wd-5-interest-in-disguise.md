## LinkedIn

The uncomfortable truth about modern Islamic banking: legal compliance doesn't always equal spiritual compliance. A customer walks into an Islamic bank wanting cash. The bank buys metal on the London exchange, sells it to the customer at a markup, the customer immediately sells the metal back to the market at a loss, and walks out with cash. The metal never moved. No one wanted the metal. This is tawarruq - a practice that accounts for a significant portion of Islamic banking transactions globally. The entire transaction exists to disguise what is, economically, an interest-bearing loan. Scholars like Mufti Taqi Usmani have called organized tawarruq impermissible, yet the practice continues. When legal tricks replicate the economic substance of riba, have we honored the spirit of the prohibition or merely its letter? This raises profound questions about the future of Islamic finance and whether current practices truly serve the principles they claim to uphold.
