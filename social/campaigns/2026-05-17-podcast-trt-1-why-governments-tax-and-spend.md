## LinkedIn

Why do governments tax and spend at all? It comes down to a fundamental market failure. Private markets excel at producing goods people buy individually — phones, food, clothing. But they systematically underprovide public goods like defense, infrastructure, and clean water. The free rider problem means everyone hopes someone else will pay, making voluntary contributions inadequate at scale. The early Islamic state understood this principle remarkably well. Under Abu Bakr and Umar ibn al-Khattab, the Rashidun Caliphate built a comprehensive fiscal system from scratch: zakat collection from Muslims, kharaj taxes on agricultural land, and fay' revenue from conquered territories — all channeled through the bayt al-mal (public treasury). Their key innovation was the principle of amana: public revenue is a trust held on behalf of the people, not the property of the ruler. This concept of public stewardship emerged in 7th-century Arabia, centuries before similar ideas took hold in Europe. The caliphs solved what economists now call the free rider problem 1,400 years before we had a formal name for it. Listen: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-trt-1
