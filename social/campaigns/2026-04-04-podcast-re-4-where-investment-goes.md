## LinkedIn

Two countries with identical savings rates can have vastly different growth outcomes. The difference lies in capital allocation—how efficiently the financial system channels savings into productive investment. Capital misallocation reduces total factor productivity by 30 to 50 percent in developing countries. The financial system's efficiency in directing capital determines economic growth. Islamic economics offers a compelling alternative: equity-based financing through mudaraba and musharaka partnerships. These structures align investor incentives with productive ventures rather than simply rewarding safe borrowers with mediocre projects. Yet Islamic banking in practice hasn't delivered this transformation. Most Islamic banks rely on murabaha, which is economically similar to conventional lending. Genuine profit-loss sharing remains rare across Muslim-majority economies. The theory pointed toward better capital allocation. The practice recreated the same problems it sought to solve.
