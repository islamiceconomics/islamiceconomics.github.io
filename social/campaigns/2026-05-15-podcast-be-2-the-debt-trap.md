## LinkedIn

The Ottoman Public Debt Administration of 1881 was a turning point in sovereign finance. European creditors took direct control of Ottoman state revenues after the empire defaulted on its debts. A century later, the IMF offered structural adjustment programs to dozens of countries, including many Muslim-majority nations. The mechanics are different, but the pattern is remarkably similar: surrender policy autonomy for financial assistance. What's striking is how outcomes varied. South Korea used IMF programs as a bridge to economic transformation. Pakistan has cycled through program after program without making fundamental reforms. The difference often comes down to domestic governance and political will. The debt trap is real, but it's not just about predatory lending. It's about the choices borrowing nations make when they have room to maneuver. Listen: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-be-2
