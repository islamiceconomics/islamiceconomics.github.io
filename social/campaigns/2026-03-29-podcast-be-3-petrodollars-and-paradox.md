After the 1973 oil embargo, Gulf states faced an unprecedented financial paradox. Massive petrodollar accumulation, but limited Islamic investment options at the required scale. Their solution was pragmatic: they did both. On one hand, they channeled funds into US Treasury bonds and Western financial markets - interest-bearing instruments that sit uncomfortably with Islamic prohibitions. On the other, they funded over $150 billion in development through Islamic institutions: the Islamic Development Bank, Kuwait Fund, and Saudi Development Fund. They built infrastructure across the Muslim world while simultaneously integrating into the global financial system. This dual approach reveals the complexity of sovereign wealth management in a world where Islamic and conventional finance intersect. https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-be-3
