## LinkedIn

Government spending raises fundamental questions: What should states provide directly, and what can emerge from voluntary coordination? The Islamic historical record offers an interesting case study. For centuries, Muslim societies operated a dual model: the bayt al-mal (state treasury) funded defense, infrastructure, and poverty relief, while the waqf system created a parallel layer of public goods provision. The waqf endowments built hospitals, schools, and water infrastructure without depending on tax collection or bureaucratic approval. Wealthy individuals and communities voluntarily funded public goods that served broad social needs. Neither system was perfect. The waqf suffered from rigidity—donors locked in specific uses that couldn't adapt to changing circumstances. The Ottomans eventually captured the entire system, turning voluntary provision into another arm of state control. But the institutional design itself—combining state and non-state provision—remains worth studying. It addressed both the efficiency problems of pure state provision and the coordination problems of pure market provision. Listen: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-trt-3
