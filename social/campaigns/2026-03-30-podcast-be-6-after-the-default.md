## LinkedIn

What happens when a nation simply cannot pay its debts? Argentina has defaulted nine times. Lebanon's debt-to-GDP hit 150% before its economy collapsed. Sri Lanka defaulted in 2022 after running out of foreign reserves. The modern financial system offers few options: impose austerity, restructure the debt, or watch the economy collapse. But Islamic tradition suggests a different approach. The Quran explicitly encourages creditors to forgive debts when debtors face genuine hardship. The concept of odious debt—that populations shouldn't repay loans taken by dictators against their interests—resonates deeply with Islamic principles of justice. This perspective isn't just historical. As debt crises multiply globally, Islamic frameworks for sovereign debt resolution prioritize human welfare over rigid contractual obligations.
