## LinkedIn

When Prophet Muhammad arrived in Medina in 622 CE, he found a marketplace designed for exploitation. Clever merchants used sham bidding, hoarding, and deceptive practices to systematically disadvantage ordinary traders and consumers. His response wasn't just spiritual guidance—it was comprehensive market reform. He established open access, banned najash (artificial price inflation), prevented hoarding, and created the hisbah system: appointed market inspectors who ensured fair weights, honest dealing, and consumer protection. These weren't abstract principles. They were practical regulations that transformed how commerce operated, creating the foundation for Islamic commercial law that would govern trade across continents for centuries. What strikes me is how these 7th-century reforms anticipated modern consumer protection by more than a millennium. The core insight remains unchanged: markets need structure and oversight to serve everyone, not just the clever few. Listen: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-2
