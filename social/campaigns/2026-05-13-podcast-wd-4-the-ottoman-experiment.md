For centuries, the Ottoman Empire operated as the world's largest economy without sovereign debt. Cash waqfs provided regulated lending to merchants, wars were funded through taxation and conquest, and the empire thrived without bond markets. Then came 1854 and the Crimean War. The Ottomans issued their first foreign loan from European banks. Within twenty years, debt payments consumed over half the state's revenue. By 1881, European creditors had established the Ottoman Public Debt Administration, effectively seizing control of the empire's finances. The transition from Islamic financial principles to European-style sovereign debt became a pathway to economic colonization.
