## LinkedIn

During the caliphate of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, zakat collection was so effective that there were reportedly no eligible recipients left in parts of the empire. This wasn't charity—it was systematic wealth redistribution that actually worked. Zakat is a mandatory 2.5% annual wealth tax on assets above a minimum threshold. Modern estimates suggest that if properly collected across the Muslim world, it could generate $200-600 billion annually. That's enough to eliminate extreme poverty in Muslim-majority countries without a single dollar of foreign debt. We examine how zakat functions as an automatic stabilizer, redistributive mechanism, and potential alternative to deficit spending. From historical systems to modern experiments in Malaysia, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan—why does this 1,400-year-old fiscal policy remain largely untapped?
