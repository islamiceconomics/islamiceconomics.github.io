## LinkedIn

In 9th-century Baghdad, jahbadh bankers revolutionized commerce with a simple piece of paper called the sakk. This bill of exchange could move wealth across continents without a single gold coin traveling the route. The system relied entirely on trust networks — hawala systems and mudaraba partnerships that connected merchants from Spain to China. A trader in Baghdad could write a note that would be honored 4,000 miles away in Canton, purely on reputation. This wasn't just clever bookkeeping. It was the foundation of modern banking, created centuries before European banks existed. The word "cheque" comes directly from the Arabic "sakk." Medieval Islamic finance moved money faster than armies could march, enabling global trade on a scale that wouldn't be seen again for centuries.
