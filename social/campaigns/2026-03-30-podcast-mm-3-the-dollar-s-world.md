## LinkedIn

The dollar's global reserve status creates a peculiar form of economic sovereignty. While it provides real stability and liquidity that the world depends on, it also means that Federal Reserve decisions made with American workers in mind ripple through every economy that trades in dollars—which is almost all of them. From Bretton Woods to the Nixon shock to the petrodollar arrangement, the dollar's journey to reserve currency status wasn't inevitable. But once established, it created what economists call "exorbitant privilege"—the ability to borrow in your own currency while others must navigate exchange rate risk. For Muslim-majority economies, this system presents daily asymmetries: oil priced in dollars, debt denominated in dollars, vulnerability to monetary policy designed for different economic priorities.
