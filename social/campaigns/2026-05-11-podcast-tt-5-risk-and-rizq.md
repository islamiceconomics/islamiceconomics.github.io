## LinkedIn

The word "hazard" traces back to al-zahr — Arabic for dice. It's a perfect metaphor for how Islamic civilization gave the world its vocabulary of risk and uncertainty. Islamic contracts prohibited gharar (excessive uncertainty) not to avoid risk, but to ensure fairness. You could sell wheat growing in your field, but not wheat you merely hoped to plant next year. The distinction was crucial: legitimate business risk versus exploitative speculation. Khadijah bint Khuwaylid exemplified this merchant spirit, running trade caravans across Arabia through mudaraba partnerships that split profits between capital and labor without guaranteeing returns. This culture of ethical risk-taking evolved into modern takaful insurance, now a $50 billion industry based on risk-sharing rather than risk-transfer. From medieval trading partnerships to contemporary Islamic finance, the principles remain constant: embrace uncertainty, but structure it fairly.
