The Prophet Muhammad explicitly banned bay' al-gharar: transactions built on excessive uncertainty, where one party doesn't know what they're really buying. Early Islamic jurists considered this prohibition as fundamental as the ban on riba. Remarkably, 7th-century Islamic contract law was already addressing systemic risks that would crash modern financial markets fourteen centuries later. The principles weren't just about individual fairness—they were about protecting entire economic systems from uncertainty-based collapse.
