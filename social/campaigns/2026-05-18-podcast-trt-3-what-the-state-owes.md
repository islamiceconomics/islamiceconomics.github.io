## LinkedIn

Public goods provision doesn't have to be a binary choice between state and market. The Ottoman Empire demonstrated a sophisticated dual model that modern economists are rediscovering. The bayt al-mal (state treasury) funded defense, infrastructure, and poverty relief through taxation. Meanwhile, the waqf system created a parallel layer of voluntary public goods provision — hospitals, schools, water infrastructure — funded by religious endowments. Neither system was perfect. Waqfs eventually became rigid and suffered state capture. But the institutional design is fascinating: combining mandatory and voluntary provision, creating funding streams that could last centuries while separating different types of public goods. This historical model offers insights for contemporary debates about government spending, crowding out effects, and the role of civil society in providing public goods.
