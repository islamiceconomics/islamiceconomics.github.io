## LinkedIn

In 2014, something remarkable happened in global finance. The United Kingdom became the first Western nation to issue a sovereign sukuk, raising 200 million pounds backed by government property. This wasn't just borrowing with different paperwork. Conventional sovereign bonds are loans to governments at predetermined interest rates. Sukuk are ownership stakes in real government assets, with returns tied to what those assets actually generate. Malaysia has built the world's largest sukuk market with over $200 billion outstanding. Hong Kong, Luxembourg, and South Africa have followed suit. The model works: governments can raise capital without taking on interest-based debt, while investors own shares in tangible assets rather than IOUs. The debate continues over whether sukuk truly differ from conventional bonds in economic substance. But the practice is clear: a government can raise hundreds of millions without borrowing a single penny. Listen: https://islamiceconomics.github.io/podcast.html#ep-be-5
