## LinkedIn

One of development economics' most robust findings: countries that discover oil or minerals often end up worse off, not better. The resource curse weakens institutions, concentrates power, and triggers Dutch disease—where resource wealth crowds out productive sectors. Norway and Botswana are rare exceptions. Islamic economics anticipated this challenge. The fay' principle establishes that communal resources belong to the community, not rulers. The hima system created frameworks for protecting commons from overexploitation. Gulf sovereign wealth funds represent a partial modern application of these principles, channeling resource wealth for broader benefit. Yet no Muslim-majority resource economy has fully implemented Islamic resource governance.
